PRINCIPLE STYLE:
Write each selection criterion as one short sentence beginning with
"Select the response". Express one reusable, discriminating quality.
Use the highest-level abstraction that preserves the preference supported by the
evidence. A rule must still make sense for new inputs with different entities,
answers, dates and values. Domain concepts and task constraints may remain;
sample-specific names, titles, locations, exact answers, quantities and quotations
must not. Keep concrete facts in the rationale or debate evidence, not the rule.
Do not merely replace a name with "the person" while retaining the sample's fact.
Do not impose a new preference unsupported by the evidence, or collapse distinct
qualities into a vague rule such as "is better". Avoid lists of exceptions and
parenthetical explanations. Never turn a sample answer into a selection rule.
